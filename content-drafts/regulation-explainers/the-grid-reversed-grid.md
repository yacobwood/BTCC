# The reversed grid: how BTCC decides Race 3's starting order live

BTCC's three-race weekend format only really clicks once you understand how each grid is actually built. Sporting Regulation 3.4 lays out the chain. Its last link is the reversed grid for Race 3, which is drawn at random after Race 2 has finished, so nobody on the grid knows the full shape of Race 3 until the previous race is over. From 2027 that draw also reaches further down the field than it did in 2026.

## Three grids, three different sources

The Qualifying Race grid is set in order of qualification from Official Qualifying, up to the maximum number of starters the venue's ASN Circuit Licence allows. From 2027 that is a single 20-minute session with the whole field running together, replacing the two 15-minute groups used in 2026. From there, each grid feeds off the race before it. The Race 1 grid comes from the Qualifying Race's finishing order, as published by the official timekeepers and subject to any judicial action. Anyone not classified as a finisher in the Qualifying Race is placed behind the last classified competitor, ordered by how many laps they actually completed. The Race 2 grid works exactly the same way, built from Race 1's finishing order under the same non-finisher rule.

## Then Race 3 breaks the pattern

Race 3's grid starts from Race 2's finishing order too, but with a twist that genuinely does not exist anywhere else in the weekend. As soon as practical after Race 2 finishes, someone nominated by the Administrator picks a number at random. That many of the top Race 2 finishers then have their grid positions reversed for Race 3. From 2027 the number can be anything from 10 to 16 inclusive. In 2026 it ran from 6 to 12.

Here is what that looks like if 13 is drawn, one of seven possible outcomes:

| Race 2 Finish | Race 3 Grid (if 13 is drawn) |
|---|---|
| 1st | 13th |
| 2nd | 12th |
| 3rd | 11th |
| 4th | 10th |
| 5th | 9th |
| 6th | 8th |
| 7th | 7th |
| 8th | 6th |
| 9th | 5th |
| 10th | 4th |
| 11th | 3rd |
| 12th | 2nd |
| 13th | 1st |
| 14th and below | Unchanged |

> In other words, the field does not know how far down the order the flip will reach until after the chequered flag falls on Race 2. It could stop at tenth or run all the way to 16th.

A driver who falls inside the reversed positions but then picks up a time penalty relating to Race 2 keeps the grid slot that matches their original Race 2 finishing order, unless that penalty actually changes their finishing position enough to push them outside the reversed group entirely, in which case the amended position applies instead. Every other classified finisher is simply carried over in Race 2's finishing order, subject to the same judicial-action caveat and anyone not classified as a finisher in Race 2 goes to the back of the Race 3 grid, ordered by laps completed, exactly as with the earlier grids. All of this follows the 2026 wording of Regulation 3.4 because TOCA has not yet published the 2027 Sporting Regulations.

## What the bigger draw changes

The Race 2 winner will now start Race 3 somewhere between 10th and 16th, where in 2026 the worst case was 12th. The driver with the most riding on the draw is whoever finishes 16th. If 16 comes out they start Race 3 from pole. Any lower number leaves them exactly where they finished.

## Starting from the back with no time penalty

A driver who has not set a qualifying time at all is not locked out of the Qualifying Race. They can start from the back of the grid, behind every driver who did set a time, without any time penalty added on top. If more than one driver finds themselves in that position, they are ordered among themselves by their fastest time in the Free Practice session that preceded Official Qualifying, fastest driver first. That wording was written for a single practice session. Saturday has two 25-minute sessions from 2027, so this tie-break may be reworded when the 2027 regulations arrive.

## What happens if a session cannot be held at all

Force majeure gets its own fallback. If Official Qualifying cannot be run at all, the grid for that meeting is instead formed from the corresponding round at the previous meeting or by drawing lots where even that is not possible. If the Qualifying Race specifically cannot be held, the grid is formed straight from the Official Qualifying results instead.

## Why draw the reversal live, rather than fix it in advance

A fixed reversal, always the top 10 or always the top 16, would be predictable enough that teams could plan their Race 2 strategy entirely around it. Drawing the actual number at random and only after Race 2 has already been run keeps that calculation impossible. A driver running 13th late in Race 2 genuinely does not know whether holding that place will put them at the sharp end of the Race 3 grid or leave them exactly where they are. That uncertainty is precisely what keeps BTCC's reversed-grid format from turning into a formality everyone has already gamed out before the lights go green.
